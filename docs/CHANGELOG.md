## 2026-11-26

**New**

- The Voice helper frees GPU memory when idle and wakes on the next question — `voice`
  - *Problem:* the Voice helper kept about 5.4 GB of the 12 GB graphics card even when nobody had asked it anything for hours, which makes CAD and the desktop stutter.
  - *Method:* after idleUnloadMinutes (15, 0 = never) without a request its models are unloaded from GPU memory (the process keeps running); the next question loads them again with a "warming up" status and the wake-up cost is kept for /health. Never during a question or in presence/greeter mode. Optional yieldVramWhen (for example SLDWORKS.exe) unloads after one minute while such a program runs.
  - *Numbers:* pending a measurement on the real helper (expect about 5.4 GB freed): tools/voice_idle_measure.py.
- One combined request file for the friend apps, charts viewable anywhere — `docs`
- PRs with the auto-merge label now merge themselves once the quick checks pass — `tools`
  - *Problem:* the owner had to type a merge approval for every PR.
  - *Method:* label `auto-merge` on a ready PR; GitHub Actions squash-merges it when the light CI check `quick-qc` passes. Only owner-added labels on same-repo branches count.

**Improved**

- GPU-memory planner measured on the real PC: card sizes 2 to 12 GB and spill vs swap vs hybrid — `voice`
  - *Problem:* nobody knew how the planner behaves on a small card, or whether moving jobs to the CPU or taking turns on the GPU is better.
  - *Method:* ran the whole voice helper on this PC with a pretend card of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 GB and the whole card, with the face app, camera and background jobs running, and compared three ways of using the CPU.
  - *Numbers:* hearing takes about 4 s with models on the CPU (2 to 6 GB) and about 1 s from 8 GB; the planner as shipped wins; at 6 GB it keeps the wrong hearing model on the CPU; the "hybrid" way makes hearing 3 times slower. See docs/perf_vram_2026-11-26.md.
- QC, builds and measurement scripts now run at low priority so the PC stays usable — `qc`
  - *Problem:* a full QC run left the PC laggy and hard to use, and other programs are open in real use.
  - *Method:* QC (runner, workers, Edge launched through PowerShell, subprocesses), the exe build wrapper and our measurement scripts run at below-normal CPU, low disk and low memory priority inside a job object with a CPU cap and a memory limit. A governor watches the whole machine every 2 seconds and starts no new check while CPU is above 75%, free RAM below 6 GB or the GPU is busy (one check per pool always runs, nothing is stopped or killed). Off: MICE_POLITE=0, --exact-timing or "polite": false (the tool says it runs at normal priority). Optional stronger yield in qc/data/qc_speed.json. The real hub, Voice and Faces are never touched.
  - *Numbers:* see docs/experiments/experiment-records.md (2026-11-26 polite mode).
- Coordination notes mention the auto-merge label — `tools`

**Fixed**

- The GPU planner now puts the model that saves the most time on the GPU, and waits for a busy model instead of moving to the CPU — `voice`
  - *Problem:* measured 2026-11-26 on the PC: at 6 GB the small hearing model loaded first and kept the GPU while the Thai one (saves 2.5 s per visitor) ran on the CPU; at 10 GB a busy local AI sent a newcomer to the CPU, so a bigger card had fewer models on the GPU than 8 GB.
  - *Method:* an equally important model is replaced only by one that saves more time than everything it pushes out; a busy model is asked again until its idle moment (8 s, config) before the newcomer falls back to the CPU; the Thai hearing size uses the measured saving (2877 ms CPU vs 422 ms GPU).
  - *Numbers:* a QC check now walks 2 to 16 GB in 256 MiB steps and fails if a bigger card ever saves less time. Re-measurement: docs/perf_vram_2026-11-26.md.
- QC no longer leaves headless browsers running after a crash, Ctrl+C or a timed-out check — `qc`
  - *Problem:* 119 headless Edge processes from QC runs stayed alive on the PC (up to 67 h old, about 4 GB of memory) after killed runs, crashes and timed-out checks.
  - *Method:* the runner joins a Windows job object that kills everything it started when it ends (even if killed hard); Ctrl+C and SIGTERM end the run cleanly; every run starts by killing browsers whose owner is gone or older than 6 h (never a live run's); a full run fails if it leaves a browser behind. Proven with real processes in check_no_orphans.
  - *Numbers:* a hard-killed runner now takes its child and a detached grandchild with it within 15 s (was: they lived on).

**Improved**

- QC faster and steadier on the PC (#252, #253, #258, #260, #261, #262, #264, #265, #268) — `qc`
  - *Problem:* Full QC took 1524 s on the PC and failed randomly under parallel load (checks overwrote each other's saves, the QC hub met the real hub).
  - *Method:* Per-check timing, watchdog with hard timeout, build caches, a PC-wide run lock, timing-sensitive checks moved into the pool, each check gets its own projects/sequences/shows copy, QC hub kept away from the real hub.
  - *Numbers:* Full QC 1524 s to 832 s; Video LAN driver check 233 s to 22 s.
- Friend requests and QC notes (docs only) (#255, #263, #266, #267, #257) — `docs`
  - *Problem:* Friend-facing notes were out of date; flake findings were unrecorded.

  - *Numbers:* see docs/experiments/2026-11-26_f1_batch_embedding
- All apps together: Whisper on GPU, shared priority scheduler (#251, #259) — `performance`
  - *Problem:* Voice, face recognition and cameras competed for the GPU; live work could wait behind background work.
  - *Method:* Whisper back on GPU, early face lookup on /ask; one shared scheduler (live jumps the queue, background yields, aging prevents starvation), classes and limits in config/scheduler.json.
  - *Numbers:* see docs/experiments/2026-11-25_all_apps_together.md
