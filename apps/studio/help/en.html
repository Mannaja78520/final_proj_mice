Automatic dance separation: The default, Automatic, detects several people and camera angle changes in a new clip by itself. With Automatic or Group dance, the system compares the movement across the whole clip every time, and does not fix male/female positions in advance. People whose arms are the same but whose waist twist or shoulder tilt differ may end up in different routines. Choose Dance routine to see each one, or Save separate routines to save, as separate projects, the routines with continuously different evidence and reliable pictures through the picture ratio set in the defaults. A Fragment is a stretch that cannot yet be confirmed as a new routine; it is kept to view and save one at a time, and every camera cut is not counted as a new dance routine. If a new angle cannot be matched clearly to an earlier routine, the system uses the dancers' appearance together with matching every group at once, and marks check cut when the match is still unclear. A new angle whose match is still unclear keeps the pose and is not turned into reliable just because the time has passed. Only when several people seen in that picture really do the same pose is the confirmed shared pose used. The % reliable report is the share of usable pictures, not a guarantee that it is the same person. Users may choose people themselves as an option.

A cut that cannot be matched to the same person: The system automatically splits it into scene segments and picks the main dancer who is seen continuously in that routine, not switching with every picture's confidence value. Each part's video range and move timing start in line. When you edit the video range, the earlier reading is cancelled and the new range is analysed, without adding a long standing-still before the visible stretch. Make and save saves the routines or ranges that pass the criteria separately, and does not create a show that guesses different moves are the same person. An unclear cross-camera match is not a guarantee of gender or of the same person.

Continuous dancing: The default keeps the shape of the pose and adds time according to the robot's limits. To keep the original musical beat, choose Stay with the music, which may reduce the range of movement. The system filters jitter over time, cutting short reversals and sudden joint-angle changes. When a person holds still after changing pose, the robot still moves to the pose it accepted before holding, and does not stop halfway because of the filter. The opening pose and the standing-still stretches are kept, and no pose from the future is pulled in to fill pictures that cannot be seen. After filtering, collisions and timing are checked again and the number of reversals before and after is shown. The original pictures and the error report are still used to check whether filtering has eaten too much pose detail. The rig and the motor limits may leave some poses still different from the person.

Turn the waist uses the torso angle relative to the hips, and Follow shoulder tilt uses the shoulder angle relative to the torso, checked against the real mechanism in the rig. A person who stands still does not pull in a future pose to fill in, and nothing is smoothed across a cut. A side or overhead camera is checked within the torso frame before a new pose is accepted.

Learned camera checks uses a Q-learning policy trained in a simulator to help decide whether to accept a pose, keep the old pose, or compensate for the viewpoint. It is compared with the geometric method before being switched on. If the person is blocked, it is not certain it is the same person, or the person's rotation cannot be separated from the camera's, it keeps the last reliable pose. The simulator results do not confirm accuracy on real clips.

Checks the hand plates, joint balls, arm rods, torso and head against the real pose, including the shoulder lift. Nong Studio — a user guide and every tuning point in detail

A 3D simulator and animation pose designer for the Nong humanoid robot (10 servo joints). It runs on a PC, with on-demand 3D rendering that saves power, takes little from the machine and does not freeze.

Overview of the current Nong Studio screen (Current Real UI) Overview
Point 1: the panel for adjusting the 10 servo joint angles (Joint Sliders & Inspector) Joint Controls
  1. Move the joint sliders on the Pose tab and watch the 3D preview.
  2. Use Neutral pose to reset the preview. Mirror L↔R swaps sides. Mirror front↔back flips each arm's reach without swapping arms.
Point 2: the timeline and keyframe sequencer (Timeline & Keyframe Sequencer) Timeline
1 Play a sequence (Play / Run on robot)
How to set it: Press the ▶ Play to preview on screen, or ▶ Run on robot to command the real robot
What changes on the robot: The 3D model and the real robot move continuously through the keyframes, as smoothly as the speed curve that was calculated.
2 Saving a pose (+ Add Keyframe & Duration)
How to set it: Bend the pose you want, then press + Add keyframe with a duration (for example 800 ms)
What changes on the robot: to add a new pose to the timeline. If the time is too short for the servo to turn in, the system stretches it automatically to stop the motor from jerking.
3 Scrub bar (Scrub Bar & Live Tracking)
How to set it: Drag the time pointer forward or back
What changes on the robot: The robot follows the dragged point in time in real time, so you can check exactly where parts of the structure collide.
4 Loop and zoom (Loop Checkbox)
How to set it: Tick the box Loop
What changes on the robot: When playback reaches the last pose, the robot moves smoothly back to the first pose and plays again continuously, with no jerking or arm flick.
Point 3: the 3D model and Inverse Kinematics control (3D Canvas) 3D & IK
  1. Use Top, Left, Front, Right, Back or Bottom to see the model from that direction. Select 3D to orbit.
  2. On the Pose tab, drag a joint in the preview or use its slider.

To keep a card open, press ⧉ on the card header. It floats out as its own window. Drag the header anywhere, drag the bottom-right corner to resize it, and it stays visible on every tab. Press ⇲ to put it back. The browser remembers the position (there is no such button on a phone).

  1. Open Setup, then expand Servo offset. Use − or + to nudge a joint by 0.5°, enter an offset, or choose 0 for that joint.
  2. Joint limits and the shrug curve are in the separate Rig setup card on the same tab.

  1. In Shows, choose a saved show or select New. Add saved sequences in the order you want them to run.

What changes on the robot: This page speaks the sentence in the same voice as the Voice page, and the arms in the 3D picture move with the sound. It is a preview only; the real robot does not move. Robot pose training pictures (PC experiment)

Generate pose views with python -m tools.robot_pose synth --n 20000 --no-images --out ABSOLUTE_PRIVATE_PATH, then render python -m tools.robot_pose.render_mujoco ABSOLUTE_PRIVATE_PATH/views.jsonl --out ABSOLUTE_PRIVATE_PATH. MuJoCo uses the Studio rig and the saved camera, random light, colour, textures, blur and noise. Labels mark keypoints hidden by another part using depth. Joint centres inside their own housing use a rig-sized tolerance. Keep all pictures, labels and reports in the PC private .local/robot_pose folder using absolute paths. These are simulated images; real video labels still need independent hold observations and visual review. CAD appearance is optional when the private meshes exist; it follows the shared rig, with source attachment offsets checked. Landmarks absent from the CAD, including its virtual base point, stay unlabelled. Training views need at least three visible joints; fully hidden views are dropped. An unlabelled base point can still bound the robot post. No training, camera capture or robot commands run from this tool.

For recorded still frames, python -m tools.robot_pose.lab_labels ABSOLUTE_MANIFEST.json --out ABSOLUTE_PRIVATE_DATASET fits a camera from independently measured joint centres and known holds. The private manifest selects settled windows, validated landmarks, robot bounds, video-level train/validation splits and observed pose acceptance. A command acknowledgement alone does not prove the robot reached the pose: moving or visibly wrong frames are dropped. Only validated landmarks receive labels. Review at least 50 frame overlays before training; a single planar pose does not establish metric camera calibration.
