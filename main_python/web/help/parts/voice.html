🎤 Voice

Ask the rig a question

The Voice app in the Tools list: type a question, the rig answers. The answers come from a helper program that runs on this PC next to the hub (the hub itself never carries an AI — it only passes the question along and the answer back).

Speak instead of typingnew

The Voice page has a Press to talk button: press it, speak, press again to stop, and your words appear in the box ready to send. The listening runs on this PC too. Which languages it hears — and the words each language gets wrong often — live in config/voice.json; adding a language is one entry there.

The rig speaks one thing at a timenew

Everything the rig says goes through one queue on the hub: a greeting, an answer from this page spoken through a robot, a message from another program on this PC. So two of them never talk over each other. Something that arrives while the rig is talking either waits its turn or is skipped — whoever asked chooses — and a greeting that waited too long is dropped, because the person has walked on (maxAgeSeconds, 30 seconds unless changed).

Where it speaks. Speakers are found by themselves: every robot with a speaker that the hub reaches over WiFi, and this PC's own speakers. A robot reached only by cable is listed with that reason, and a robot whose board has no amplifier wired says so after its first try — plug one in and it is asked again. Which one is the default, and which voice each uses, is config/speakers.json; the hub writes it when somebody picks on a screen.

Live sound wins. Somebody sending sound to a robot (Send sound to the robot on its page) takes the speaker; greetings wait until it has been quiet for two seconds. If the rig's own speech takes a speaker back, the sending page stops and says so instead of sending into nothing.

Stop All silences it all — what is being said, everything waiting, and live sound — with no login, like every other stop.

In conversation mode this page waits for the robot to finish before it listens again, and throws away anything it heard while the robot was talking, so it never answers its own voice.

For a program on this PC: POST /api/speak with JSON {text, to, voice, lang, whenBusy: "queue"|"skip", take, move, module, maxAgeSeconds} needs no login when it comes from this PC with Content-Type: application/json and no browser headers; from the network, or from a web page, it needs one. GET /api/speak shows what is said and what waits (login), GET /api/speakers lists the speakers, POST /api/speak/stop is quiet now. The voice helper itself answers programs only: a web page, or any address that is not an IP or localhost, is refused.

It can greet you by namenew

Beside the answer box there is a face badge and two buttons. Check who I am uses the camera of the device the page is open on: it takes one picture, asks the face app who it is, and shows the name. The picture is not kept — nothing is written to the face app's history and no image lands on the disk. The name is remembered for two minutes, so the next answers greet that person by name in Thai, English, Japanese or Chinese.

That promise has to come from the face app itself. Before sending anything the page asks the face app whether it can look without keeping; a face app that cannot (the current Reconize cannot) is sent no picture at all, and the line under the camera says so and the looking stops. Recognition then still works through a camera the face app watches, or through Open Reconize. When the face app learns to look without keeping, this button works again by itself.

Next to it, Check by itself keeps looking every few seconds, so nobody has to press anything — still keeping no pictures. It stops while the tab is hidden, and it lets the camera go the moment it is switched off. How often it looks is face.autoSeconds in config/voice.json.

The list beside the switch holds two kinds of camera. Cameras the face app watches are the stations it already runs — its kiosk, a CCTV camera, a phone, or a PC watching several cameras at once. Picking one of those opens no camera here: the face app is already looking through that lens, and Voice simply reads what it sees. That is how one camera serves both programs at the same time. Under This device are the cameras of the machine the page is open on, for when no station is running.

It starts on the camera the face app saved as its own default, so choosing there chooses here too; picking another is remembered in this browser only, the same way the face app's kiosk remembers its own.

How fast a name shows. Names come from the face app's live camera through the rig's face watcher, so a face shows here about as fast as the face app sees it. When the watcher is not running, the page starts it by itself (only the watcher, never the face app); until then names come from the face app's history, which is slow.

👤 Use faces at the start of that row turns face recognition off altogether: no camera, no names, nothing waited on, and every answer comes the plain way. Tick it again to bring faces back. It is saved for the rig, not only this browser.

Show the camera keeps a live picture of the chosen camera under the row. This device's camera is shown only here; a camera on the rig comes through the hub, the same picture the face watcher reads. A camera only the face app knows shows its picture in the face app, so the line says to use Open Reconize. Showing sends and keeps nothing, and the picture stops while the tab is hidden.

Open Reconize opens the face app's own camera page instead, in a new tab, and starts the face app first if it is not running. That way keeps a record of every sighting, which is what an event needs; the button above is for a quick hello. Where the face app lives is one entry in config/partners.json — a second face app is an entry there, not new code.

Saved answers firstnew

A question close to a saved one is answered instantly from apps/voice/qa_data.json — the same trick as the original prototype, and the part that works with no AI at all. Add or change an answer by editing that file; the helper reads it fresh every time, so there is nothing to restart.

If a question is only somewhat close to a saved one, the rig does not guess — it asks the visitor to say it again. A confident wrong answer is worse than asking twice.

A saved answer can also carry a move: give the entry a move (a saved sequence file) and a module (which robot plays it — its name as the modules screen shows it), and the robot performs that sequence when the answer is given. The name is looked up at play time, so the robot can be plugged into any PC without editing anything; if no robot of that name is connected, the page says so plainly. While that robot is already moving, another ask is answered in words — it never queues and never cuts the running move. A Stop the move button stays beside it until the sequence ends.

Ask for a language, a reading or a new poemnew

Say "Please answer in English" (or ตอบเป็นภาษาไทย, 英語で答えて, 用中文回答) anywhere in the question, and the answer comes in that language, even when the rest of the question is in another one.

Say "Read this aloud: …" (or ช่วยอ่าน: …, 読んでください：…, 请朗读：…) and the rig reads exactly those words, with nothing added.

Ask it to write a new poem (แต่งกลอนใหม่ …, "write a poem about …") and it makes up a new one instead of reciting the saved poem. If the answer does not look like a poem (a list, a greeting, too few lines, the old poem), it says sorry honestly instead. The small model on this PC usually cannot write a good Thai poem yet; pick a stronger service for Writing verse on the Voice page once you have its key. The shape of the poem is checked, its rhyme and rhythm are not.

English words spoken inside Thai, like parking or restroom, still find their saved answers when the listener writes them in Thai letters. When a Thai greeting is followed by an English question, the rig listens to it a second time in English, but only if that part really sounds English, so a Thai question is never answered in English. Rules, phrases and loanwords are data in config/voice_intents.json (replyLanguage, readAloud, compose, loanwords, codeSwitch); the second hearing adds one listening pass only when no saved answer fits.

One brain, many PCsnew

The answering helper normally listens to this PC only. To make one PC the brain for every hub at the venue, write that PC's address in service in config/voice.json there (instead of 127.0.0.1) and point every other hub's own config/voice.json at it. Sharing is opt-in by editing, never automatic — and the tradeoff is written next to the setting: the helper itself has no password, so anything on the venue WiFi could then ask it or make it speak. Moving a robot stays safe: that still needs a login at whichever hub you use.

Out loudnew

The rig can also speak its answer. Turn on 🔊 Sound here on the Voice page to hear it in the browser too; the choice is remembered. Saved answers are prepared ahead of time, so they sound instantly. When the online voice cannot be reached, this PC's own voice stands in for that line only, and the good voice is used again as soon as it answers. While it stands in, a yellow line under the status says so and why (for example: the natural voice is not installed on this PC, or the PC is offline).

Speaking manner: polite, friendly, playful, sweet, sassy, poem recitalnew

On the Voice page, Speaking manner picks how the rig says its lines; ▶ Try plays a sample first. "As now" keeps today's voice. An event (scenario) can have its own manner. The words of a line are never changed.

Sassy (ฟาดๆ แบบเจ๊) is a cheeky big-sister manner: a sharper voice, and new answers from the AI call the robot เจ๊ and end with ค่ะ, teasing a little but never rude to a visitor. Saved answers keep their words; only their ending (ครับ to ค่ะ) and the robot's word for itself follow the manner. The small AI on this PC follows the ending reliably, the sassy wording only sometimes.

Thai voice with feeling (this PC) says Thai lines with a real feeling: friendly and playful sound happy, polite is calm, sweet uses a soft sad tone (the nearest it knows), all in the robot's own voice. It runs on this PC's graphics card with no internet and no key, is tried first while a manner is on, and starts by itself (about 20 seconds after the helper starts; until then the usual voice speaks). It makes a 5-second line in about 3 seconds, then saved answers play at once. It speaks Thai only; English lines go to the usual voice. Its model may not be used commercially. Install once: python apps/voice/tts_spark/setup.py (3.8 GB, into .local/tts_spark, not in git); it listens on 127.0.0.1:8779. Words were checked by hearing them back (0.87-1.0 alike); whether the feeling is right needs a human ear.

What a manner really does depends on the speaking service, and the page says which: a service that takes a manner in words (Google Gemini voice, with its key) changes the voice itself. The free Microsoft voices can change only speed and pitch, so they sound lighter or slower, not truly emotional, and a poem recital there is a slow reading, not a real ทำนองเสนาะ tune. This PC's own voice cannot change at all. A manner is never dropped silently: the quick device voice is not used while one is chosen. Manners are data in config/voice_styles.json; each service's ability is the speak job's styles (instruct / emotion / prosody / none) in config/voice_providers.json; /say answers X-Voice-Style with how it was applied.

Teach it new answersnew

The Settings under the question are tabs, one topic each:

Answers is a sheet, like a spreadsheet: one row per answer, one column per language, with the ways people ask it on top (one per line) and the reply under them. Click a cell and type — nothing moves to a form somewhere else. ＋ Add a row puts a new row on top, ▶ plays a cell, 🌐 Translate fills a row's empty languages, and a language left empty is translated when you save. Changed rows are marked; Save answers keeps them all, Undo changes puts back what is saved. A robot move is a robot and one of its saved sequences, both picked from a list.

Voice — one voice that speaks every language (best: a Thai name inside an English answer is still read), or a voice per language, with ▶ Try beside each, plus speed and pitch. Listening — fast / balanced / most accurate, and the words it often mishears. Personality — what the rig is being (a scenario, which may have its own voice and speed), its polite word and what it calls itself. Faces — how long a name is remembered. The helper's address and the AI model are under Advanced, behind the Show technical details switch.

How it speaks Thainew — the small model that answers when nothing is saved used to write uneven Thai: it ended one sentence ครับ and the next ค่ะ, called itself ผม then ฉัน, stopped in the middle of a word, and now and then answered a Thai question in English. The Personality tab fixes the first two by choosing one of each and changing anything else it writes, gives Thai more room to finish a sentence (Thai costs two to four times more room than English, which is why the setting that suited English cut Thai short), and sets what the rig says when it has nothing to say.

Three things happen to every answer before it is spoken: an answer that is still unfinished is cut back to its last whole sentence, an answer in the wrong language is asked for again and then given up on, and an answer whose only Thai is the polite ending is treated as no answer at all. In every one of those cases the rig says it does not know and points at a member of staff. That is on purpose: it never invents a password, a price, a time, a floor, a place or an event. The most reliable way to improve what it says is not this tab — it is adding the answer under Answers, because a saved answer always wins.

Saving needs the same login as everything that can change the rig. A change reaches the running helper at once — nothing to restart, no file to edit by hand.

Starting the helper

If the page says the voice helper is not running, start it from the code folder:

python apps\voice\service.py

Everything about it lives in config/voice.json: where it answers, how close a question must be to count as saved, and which model and voice to use. Asking is behind the same login gate as every other command that can move the rig.
